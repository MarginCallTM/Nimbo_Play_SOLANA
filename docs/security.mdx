<Warning>
  Nimbo Arena is deployed on **Solana devnet**. Devnet SOL has no monetary value,
  so nothing currently at stake is worth stealing. This page describes the
  posture we are building toward, and is deliberately explicit about what is not
  in place yet.
</Warning>

## Where security actually lives here

This product has two very different halves, and they fail in different ways.

The **escrow** is a Solana program. Its guarantees are enforced by the chain and
hold even against an attacker who controls our infrastructure: bounded payouts,
no double settlement, no early round closure, and a vault with no private key.
Those are covered in detail in [Trust model](/trust-model).

The **game** is an authoritative server, and that is where the real attack
surface is. Everything the chain cannot see — who died, who ate what, what a
score is worth — is decided there.

## What the authoritative server does defend against

Because the server owns the simulation and never trusts the client's version of
events, the classic client-side cheats do not work. A modified client can ask for
anything it likes; it does not get to assert its own position, speed, or score.
Inputs are validated, not applied on faith.

The entry path is hardened separately from the game: sign-in is domain-bound, so
a signature harvested on a lookalike site does not authenticate against ours, and
deposit signatures are consumed permanently and durably — a restart does not
reopen a window for reusing one.

There is also a minimum stake. It exists for balance reasons rather than
security ones, but it closes the door on the cheapest form of griefing.

## What it does not defend against

<Warning>
  **Bots that genuinely play well are not a solved problem, and we do not claim
  they are.** An authoritative server stops *mechanical* cheating. It cannot tell
  a very good player from a very good program, because at the network level there
  is nothing to tell apart: both sign in properly, both stake real value, both
  send plausible inputs.
</Warning>

Our position is that a bot which actually plays the game — risks its stake,
hunts, dodges, and dies — is a legitimate participant. It pays the same fee as
everyone else and its mistakes feed the map like anyone else's.

What is not tolerated is abuse of the mechanics rather than skill at them:
exploiting the rules to become effectively untouchable, coordinating multiple
accounts against a single human, or feeding one account information it should not
have. These are handled as market abuse would be — policed as behaviour, not
banned as a category.

Beyond that, the honest list of what remains open:

- **Multi-account identity.** Nothing today makes one person one player. Wallets
  are cheap to create.
- **The server's word is final.** See [Trust model](/trust-model) — this is the
  structural limitation of the whole design, not a bug to be patched.
- **Single-signer settlement.** One key currently authorises payouts.

## Before mainnet

The devnet deployment is a testbed. Moving to real money requires work that has
not started, and we would rather publish the list than imply it is already done.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Adversarial audit of the entire stack">
    A dedicated red-team pass whose explicit goal is to **break this system**,
    not to review it politely. Scope is deliberately everything, not just the
    contract: the escrow program, the authoritative game server as a trusted
    oracle, the full money path from deposit through settlement to payout, key
    custody, replay protection, and the front end.

    We are planning to run this as an adversarial evaluation using frontier
    models, tasked with finding server-side exploits before anyone with worse
    intentions does. The point of scheduling it *before* mainnet is that on
    devnet a finding costs a fix, and on mainnet it costs players' money.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Key custody and multi-signature settlement">
    Move the authority key out of a single machine into managed custody, and
    require multiple signers for settlement, so that compromising one host is no
    longer enough to redirect payouts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="On-chain verification of game outcomes">
    Have the server attest to its results with a signature the program itself
    verifies, so the escrow stops accepting any well-formed amount from a trusted
    caller. This is the change that structurally reduces how much trust the
    server holds.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Mainnet is gated on this work, not on a date.

## Reporting a vulnerability

If you find a way to take value you are not owed, to make the server report a
result it should not, or to reach anything on our infrastructure you should not
be able to reach, we want to hear about it before anyone else does.

<Note>
  **A disclosure address is being set up and will be published here.** Until it
  is, please do not post working exploit details publicly — reach out through the
  contact channel listed on [nimboplay.dev](https://nimboplay.dev) and we will
  take the conversation somewhere private.
</Note>

What we ask: give us a reasonable window to fix an issue before disclosing it,
and do not test against other players' funds. What you can expect in return:
we will not pursue anyone acting in good faith under those terms, and we will
credit you if you want the credit.

There is no bug bounty programme yet. Saying otherwise would be an easy promise
to make and a bad one to break.
