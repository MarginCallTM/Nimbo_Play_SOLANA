<Warning>
  Nimbo Arena runs on **Solana devnet**, where SOL has no monetary value. This
  page describes the design as it stands, so that the trade-off is understood
  before it ever holds anything worth losing.
</Warning>

## Start with the uncomfortable part

**The game does not run on Solana. It runs on our servers.**

A real-time arcade game needs to resolve collisions dozens of times a second for
every player at once. No blockchain can do that, so the arena is an authoritative
game server: it owns the simulation, decides who died, decides what you ate, and
decides what your score is worth when you extract.

That makes our server a **trusted oracle over real balances**. It is the single
most important thing to understand about this product, and we would rather state
it plainly than let you discover it later.

This is a genuine departure from how our lottery side-feature works. That one is
*provably fair* — the critical state lives on-chain and anyone can verify the
draw. The arena cannot make that claim, and does not.

## What the chain guarantees regardless

The Solana program never sees the game. It handles escrow, authorised settlement
and replay protection — nothing else. But within that narrow scope its guarantees
hold **even if our servers were completely compromised**, because they are
enforced by the runtime rather than by our good behaviour.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="We cannot pay out more than the vault holds" icon="scale-balanced">
    Every settlement checks the amount against the vault's actual balance, minus
    the rent-exempt floor, and fails if it exceeds it. An attacker holding our
    keys could not drain more than players deposited, and could not manufacture
    value that was never staked.

    Each round has its **own** vault, and rooms are pinned to a single round for
    their whole life. Value can move between players inside one vault, never
    across two — so one round can never be made insolvent to pay another.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="We cannot pay the same extraction twice" icon="copy">
    Every settlement creates an `ExtractReceipt` account whose address is derived
    from the round id and a nonce. Creation is an initialisation, so a second
    attempt at the same nonce hits an account that already exists and the whole
    transaction fails.

    Replay protection is therefore a property of Solana's account model, not of
    our bookkeeping. Our server cannot forget, and cannot be tricked into
    double-paying by being restarted or run twice.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="We cannot close a round early to freeze your payout" icon="lock">
    Closing a round is refused until its end timestamp has actually passed. Not
    even the authority key can bring that forward. A compromised server cannot
    slam the door on extractions that are still owed.

    In the other direction, settlements stay payable while a round is draining —
    a legitimate extraction that lands just after the deadline still gets paid.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Nobody holds the vault's keys, because it has none" icon="key">
    The vault is a program-derived address owned by the System Program. It has no
    private key that could be stolen, and we are not its owner, so we cannot
    debit it directly. Lamports leave it only through the arena program's own
    instructions, with the program itself signing for the address.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The fee destination is fixed when the round opens" icon="receipt">
    The treasury address and the fee rate are written into the round account at
    creation and are read from it on every entry. They cannot be quietly changed
    for a round that is already running and already holding player money.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What it does not guarantee

Everything above bounds *how much* can leave the vault and *who* can be paid
twice. None of it bounds **whether the number was right in the first place.**

- **The server decides your score.** If it reported that you extracted less than
  you had earned, or that you died when you did not, the chain would have no way
  to know. Any amount within the vault's balance is a valid settlement as far as
  the program is concerned.
- **One key signs the payouts.** In the current design the game server *is* the
  authority that signs settlements. Compromise that key and you compromise who
  gets paid — bounded by the guarantees above, but real.
- **Uptime and honesty are operational, not cryptographic.** You are trusting our
  operations, not only our code.

We are not aware of this being exploited, and the devnet deployment holds nothing
of value. That is not the same as a proof, and we are not going to dress it up as
one.

## How to check us

The program is deployed and every entry, settlement and round closure emits an
event. You do not have to take our word for the numbers.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Program** | `8qGdXYu3prvvvhCEfMnatdv5BAQyVL5NWsh7v5esEF2d` |
| **Cluster** | Solana devnet |
| **Round account** | seeds `["round", round_id]` |
| **Vault** | seeds `["vault", round_id]` |
| **Extraction receipt** | seeds `["extract", round_id, nonce]` |
| **Food reserve** | seeds `["food_reserve"]` |

Events emitted: `Joined` (round, player, stake, spawn value), `Extracted` (round,
player, amount, nonce) and `RoundEnded` (round, amount swept).

Useful things to verify yourself, on any explorer:

- your own deposit and payout transactions, by wallet address;
- that a round's vault balance is consistent with what went in and what was paid
  out;
- that when a round ends, whatever is left is swept into the food reserve rather
  than to us — unclaimed value is recycled into future rounds' pellets, not
  pocketed.

## Where this is going

The current arrangement is the honest MVP, not the destination. Before this
system is ever pointed at real money, the trust concentrated in our server has to
be broken up:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Adversarial audit">
    A full red-team pass over the whole stack — escrow program, authoritative
    server, the deposit and settlement path, key custody — before mainnet.
    See [Security](/security).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Key custody and multisig">
    Move the authority key into managed custody and put multiple signers behind
    settlement, so that a single compromised machine is no longer sufficient.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Signed game outcomes">
    Have the server sign its results and verify those signatures on-chain, so the
    program stops accepting any well-formed amount and starts checking an
    attestation instead. This is the change that would genuinely reduce how much
    you have to trust us.
  </Step>
</Steps>

None of these are done. They are listed here so that the gap between what exists
today and what mainnet would require is visible rather than implied.
